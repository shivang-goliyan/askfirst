# Baseline recovery, 12 October 2026

First run of the experiment in `docs/METRIC.md`. Scorer is the naive Bayes in
`askfirst/ddxplus.py`, fitted on 4,000 DDXPlus validation patients. No GPU, no language model.

Command:

    python3 -I scripts/run_recovery.py data/ddxplus/release_validate_patients 150

| | recovery | 95% CI (Wilson) | chance | lift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| top-1 | 8.0% | [4.6%, 13.5%] | 0.48% | **16.7x** |
| top-3 | 14.7% | [9.9%, 21.2%] | 1.43% | **10.2x** |
| top-10 | 27.3% | [20.8%, 35.0%] | 4.78% | 5.7x |

150 cases, 223 candidate fields per case, one evidence deleted per patient at random, seed 7.
Scorer sanity check: 65.3% top-1 pathology accuracy on 1,000 held-out patients across 49
pathologies, against a 2% chance baseline.

## Against the pre-registered bar

`docs/METRIC.md` set the kill criterion at **top-3 recovery of 30% by the end of 14 October**.
This run is at **14.7%**, which is under half of it. Said plainly because that is what
pre-registering a number is for.

Two things are true at once. The bar is not met. And the signal is unambiguous — 10x to 17x over
chance, with the lower bound of every interval well clear of the baseline, so the ranking is
finding something real rather than reordering noise.

What this run is not: it is the floor. A naive Bayes over 223 independent evidences is the
cheapest scorer that could possibly work, deliberately so, because it establishes what the open
model has to beat. Nothing here says the bar is unreachable; it says the baseline does not reach
it.

## The finding that changes the architecture

The sweep took 74 seconds for 150 cases. Each case scores 223 candidate fields across their
possible values, which is roughly 450 scorer calls, so about 67,500 evaluations in 74 seconds.
Naive Bayes is microseconds per call, so this is free.

A language model is not. At an optimistic 50 ms per forward pass, 450 calls is **22 seconds of
GPU time per case**, and the evaluation alone is thousands of cases. That does not fit in 150
GPU-hours, and it certainly does not fit in a live request.

So the exhaustive sweep cannot be the serving path. The design has to be two-stage:

1. **Propose** — a cheap scorer shortlists perhaps 20 candidate fields out of 223.
2. **Ablate** — the open model measures influence on the shortlist only, about 40 forward passes,
   roughly two seconds.

Which means the naive Bayes is not only a baseline. It is the first stage of the real system, and
the recall of its shortlist becomes a metric in its own right: if the true field is not in the
proposed 20, no amount of model quality downstream can recover it.
